Karl Bushby Walks the World

Hull, England —

On November 2, Karl Bushby finally arrived back home – after walking 31,000 miles (50,000 kilometers) and crossing through 30 different countries. He had completed the challenge of walking all the way from South America to Hull, England. It took him 28 years.

Mr. Bushby’s journey began with a bet that he could walk from the bottom of South America all the way home. From his time as a soldier, he knew that he enjoyed traveling. In 1998, he set out from Punta Arenas, Chile. At the time, Mr. Bushby was 29 years old, and he thought the journey would take about 12 years.

He had two very strict rules. First, he couldn’t use any vehicles to move forward. Second, he couldn’t return home until he arrived on foot. If he ran into trouble, he had to solve the problem himself. This means his journey wasn’t just a test of walking, but a test of survival.

In the early years, things were difficult. Mr. Bushby started his journey with just $500. He found his way using paper maps. He often slept in a tent or stayed in the homes of generous strangers. Sometimes, he even had to find food on the side of the road.

The trip was full of extreme challenges. He crossed mountains, and traveled through jungles, deserts, and even areas where fighting was going on.

One of the biggest difficulties was crossing the Bering Strait – a narrow strip of water between Alaska and Russia. Whether this could be done on foot was the central question of the challenge. Mr. Bushby crossed during the winter, climbing over large chunks of floating ice to get across.

Arriving in Russia should have been a celebration. Instead, he was put in jail for 57 days for not entering the country the correct way. Later in his journey, Mr. Bushby made a 31-day swim across the Caspian Sea to avoid traveling through Russia or Iran.

On land, Mr Bushby usually travels with a cart in which he carries everything he needs. Despite the challenges, Mr. Bushby says his trip has been “a series of miracles.” As he made progress, people along the way always helped out. Despite the challenges, Mr. Bushby says his trip has been “a series of miracles.” As he made progress, people along the way always helped out. He received donations from supporters and help from various companies. He wrote a book about his adventures, and there are plans to make a movie.

Even though he has walked alone, Mr. Bushby says he hasn’t done it by himself. “Nobody does this alone,” he says. One of the biggest lessons he’s learned is that “the world is a much kinder, nicer place than it often seems.”

On November 2, Mr. Bushby finally reached Hull, England. A large crowd of people walked with him on the final part of his trip, and celebrated as he reached his home. His mother cried as she hugged her son, who had been traveling for 28 years (above).

Mr. Bushby is now 56 years old. He’s a little nervous about what comes next, since the trip has been his life for so long. But he says he plans to “get another mission…and get busy.”
