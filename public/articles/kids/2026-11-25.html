The Case of the Mysterious Floating Island

British Columbia, Canada —

People in western Canada faced a curious mystery this summer: An island that suddenly appeared in the middle of a large lake. Then, almost as suddenly, the island seemed to disappear. Now the island has been found again, and some of the questions about it have been answered.

Williston Lake, in the Canadian province of British Columbia, is one of the world’s largest man-made reservoirs. It was created in 1972, and covers about 680 square miles (1,760 square kilometers). The reservoir is managed by a government-run company called BC Hydro. The T-shaped lake is popular with boaters, who know the lake and its islands well.

So in late July, when a boater noticed that there was an island in the lake where there hadn’t been one before, he knew something was wrong. The mystery island was large, and had trees growing on it. Pictures and videos of the mystery island were soon posted online and on social media.

Bob Gammer works for BC Hydro. When he first saw the posts about the mystery island, he wasn’t sure they were real. He thought the pictures and videos might have been created by AI.

Then he discovered a satellite image of the reservoir that had been taken on July 21. Sure enough – there was the island. And it was large. At 150 yards (137 meters) long and 75 yards (69 meters) wide, the green shape in the image was roughly the size of two football fields.

Clearly, it was a floating island. That left the questions of where the island came from and how it got there.

Mr. Gammer suggested one possible explanation: Dead logs and other pieces of wood may have built up over many years in a protected area along the reservoir’s coast. Over time, seeds may have landed on this floating mass, and plants began to grow there. As larger plants like trees grew, their roots may have helped to tie the whole structure together.

This year, the water level in the lake is the highest it’s been in 14 years. Mr. Gammer believes the high water level may have lifted the whole mass above the shoreline it was connected to. This could have broken the structure loose and turned it into a floating island.

Then, the mystery got deeper. The island seemed to have disappeared. By mid-August, it was no longer in the location where it had been before. BC Hydro began searching for the missing island, but was not having much luck.

But by the end of August, the new mystery was solved. And once more, it was a satellite image which provided the answer. The island was now pushed up against the shore on a completely different section of the lake.

With the tops of its trees catching the wind like sails, the floating island had traveled 19 miles (30 kilometers) away while no one was looking.

The floating island is now quite famous in the area. But boaters are being warned not to land on the island: its structure may be just as unstable as its location.
