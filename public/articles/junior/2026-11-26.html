China releases the first-ever AI-generated movie

It has bombed at the box office

“SANXINGDUI: FUTURE MEMORIES”, the latest sci-fi film to hit cinemas in China, features a young woman who must travel back in time to thwart the plots of an evil AI super-brain. So it is ironic that to make the movie, the filmmakers used the very technology that in the story (and possibly in the real world) threatens the destruction of mankind. “All visuals and some audio in this film were generated by artificial intelligence,” declares Sanxingdui in its title sequence.

The filmmakers did not just ask a chatbot to “make me a movie”. A crew of screenwriters, cinematographers and others spent over two years and more than 10m yuan ($1.5m) using AI to make the film. It opened on October 23rd on 10,000 screens across China—the first such AI-generated feature to get a wide release.

But it bombed at the box office. It grossed just 1.9m yuan in its first month. Audiences complain about the constant and dizzying cuts between different shots and a jarring inconsistency in visual style between scenes. Most of all, they say, the “actors” in the movie cannot act, with unnatural facial expressions that fail to convey credible emotions. Some have called it even worse than “Niu Lai”, a clunky, slow-paced animation about a cow released in August. It went viral across the country, simply for being outrageously bad.

One problem is that the technology for AI-generated videos is moving so fast. Sanxingdui’s producers threw their work away and started from scratch five times so they could catch up. Industry insiders say that AI can help to cut costs, for example taking the place of humans when making complex computer-generated imagery, but for an entire movie the quality it delivers is not yet enough to attract audiences. Ultimately, movie-goers pay for a compelling story, not the technology used to tell it.

Some AI-generated material is very popular in China. Minutes-long “micro-dramas” are all the rage. In the first half of 2026 an estimated 600m users tuned in, with 220,000 new productions on Douyin, a short-video app. AI has made it cheap to churn out such zany fare as “I Brought Guns to the Song Dynasty”. But whereas micro-dramas can be watched free, going to the cinema costs money and time, so audiences have higher expectations.

But more AI films are on the way. Bona, the production company behind “Sanxingdui”, has another three in the works. Huayi Brothers, another firm, has announced “Euler’s Identity”, a movie heavily featuring AI-generated elements. For its part, Bona has said it learned a lot from making the movie which it will take into future projects. It must hope that “Sanxingdui: Future Memories” will soon be forgotten. ■
